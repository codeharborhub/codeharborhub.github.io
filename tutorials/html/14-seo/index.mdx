Most people find a website the same way: they **type a question into a search box.** If your page is not in the results, it may as well not exist. **SEO,** short for **search engine optimization,** is the craft of making your pages easy for search engines to **find, understand, and show** to the right people.

The good news for beginners: a lot of SEO is just **good HTML.** A clear title, honest headings, meaningful elements, descriptive links, and useful content all help people, and they help search engines too. This section teaches the HTML side of SEO, one tag at a time, and is honest about what HTML **cannot** do.

## What Is SEO, Really?

SEO is **not** a trick to fool a search engine. It is the work of **helping a search engine understand your page, so it can match it with people who are looking for it.** The goals are simple:

1. **Be findable:** search engines can reach and store your pages.
2. **Be understandable:** they can tell what each page is about.
3. **Be worth clicking:** your result looks clear and trustworthy.
4. **Be worth staying on:** the page is useful, fast, and easy to use.

The fourth one matters most. A search engine's whole job is to send people to pages they will be glad they opened.

:::info Be careful with promises
Nobody can guarantee a number one ranking, and anyone who does should be treated with caution. SEO takes **weeks or months** to show results, and results depend on competition, content, and changes that you do not control.
:::

## Where HTML Helps

HTML is only one part of SEO, but it is the part **you** fully control, and the part that makes everything else work.

| HTML piece | What it does for SEO |
| :-- | :-- |
| `<title>` | Names the page in results, tabs, and bookmarks |
| `<meta name="description">` | Offers a summary that can appear under the title |
| Headings (`h1` to `h6`) | Show the outline and main topics |
| Semantic elements | Show which parts are navigation, main content, and so on |
| Links with clear text | Help crawlers find pages and understand what they are about |
| Image `alt` text | Describes images, and helps image search |
| `rel="canonical"` | Names the main version of a page that exists at several addresses |
| Robots meta | Asks search engines to index or skip a page |
| Structured data | Describes your content in a format machines read well |
| Open Graph and Twitter Cards | Control how the link looks when shared |
| `lang` and hreflang | Say which language and region a page is for |

## Where HTML Does Not Help

Honest SEO includes what is **outside** the page's HTML:

- **Content quality.** Helpful, original, accurate content that answers a real question is the biggest factor.
- **Page experience.** Fast loading, stable layout, mobile-friendly design, and HTTPS.
- **Links from other sites.** Others linking to you is a signal that your page is useful.
- **Authority and trust.** Real expertise, clear authorship, and an honest site.
- **Search intent.** Whether your page is the kind of page the searcher wanted.
- **Technical setup.** Server speed, redirects, sitemaps, and error pages.

A perfect set of tags on a thin, copied page will not get far. And a great page with missing tags can still do well. HTML is the **foundation,** not the whole building.

## White Hat and Black Hat

- **White hat SEO** follows the search engines' guidelines, and helps real people.
- **Black hat SEO** tries to trick them: hidden keywords, fake links, copied content, misleading titles, and cloaking (showing crawlers something different).

Black hat tricks may work for a little while, but they put your site at risk of being demoted or removed. They also make a worse web for readers. In this course, we only teach the first kind.

## Tools You Will Use

- **Google Search Console** (free): shows which pages are indexed, which searches find you, and any problems. Bing Webmaster Tools is the equivalent for Bing.
- **Lighthouse** (in Chrome DevTools): checks basic SEO, speed, and accessibility.
- **Rich Results Test** and **Schema Markup Validator:** test your structured data.
- **Your browser's "view source" and developer tools:** see what a crawler gets in your HTML.
- **A site search for your own pages** (the `site:` operator in many search engines) to see what is listed.

Features and names change, so check each tool's current documentation.

## Five Habits to Start Today

1. **Give every page a unique, clear title.** Say what the page is.
2. **Write for people first.** Answer one real question well.
3. **Use real headings and links.** Describe where each link goes.
4. **Add alt text** to images that carry meaning.
5. **Check what search engines see.** Use Search Console and "view source."

## SEO Checklist for Any Page

- [ ] The page has a `lang` attribute and a unique `<title>`
- [ ] There is a helpful, honest meta description
- [ ] There is one clear `h1`, and headings follow the outline
- [ ] The content is in real text, and links have descriptive text
- [ ] Images have useful `alt` text, and a sensible size
- [ ] The page has a canonical link, when needed
- [ ] The page can be crawled, unless it is meant to be private (and private pages have a login)
- [ ] Structured data is valid, and matches visible content
- [ ] Social share tags are set (Open Graph and Twitter Cards)
- [ ] The page is fast, mobile-friendly, and served over HTTPS
- [ ] Language and region are clear, if you serve several

## Security and Privacy Notes

- **`noindex` and `robots.txt` are not security.** They are polite requests to well-behaved crawlers. Pages that must stay private need a login and proper access control.
- **Do not publish private data and hope it is not found.** Search engines index what they can reach, including old test pages, staging sites, and files in public folders. Check what is public.
- **Structured data and reviews must be honest.** Fake ratings, fake reviews, or markup that does not match the visible page can lead to penalties, and it can mislead people.
- **Be careful with tracking and analytics.** Many places require consent for cookies and tracking. Collect the least data you need, and say what you collect. This is not legal advice, so check the rules that apply to you.
- **Beware of SEO scams.** Emails promising instant first-page rankings, or tools that ask for passwords or access to your Search Console, are often scams. Give out access carefully.
- **Watch for spam on your own site.** User-generated links and comments can be abused. Use `rel="ugc"` or `rel="nofollow"` where appropriate, and moderate content.

## Global Audience Notes

- **Search engines differ.** Google and Bing are common in many places, but Baidu (China), Yandex (Russia and nearby), and Naver (Korea) matter in others. Each has its own guidelines and tools, so check the ones your readers use.
- **Write in your readers' language,** and say so with the `lang` attribute. For a multilingual site, read the lesson on [International SEO](./international-seo).
- **People search in different ways.** Words, spellings, and phrases differ by language and country, so translate the **meaning,** not just the words.
- **Mobile and slow networks matter.** In many regions, most visitors use phones on slower connections. Small, fast pages do better.
- **Right-to-left languages and non-Latin scripts** need correct `lang`, `dir`, and encoding (UTF-8).
- **Laws and rules vary** by country, including privacy and advertising rules.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Writing for search engines, not people.** Stuffed, awkward text hurts everyone.
2. **The same title on every page.**
3. **No title, or "Untitled" or "Home" only.**
4. **Keyword stuffing,** including in alt text, headings, and hidden text.
5. **Relying on the meta keywords tag,** which major search engines ignore.
6. **Using headings only for size or style.**
7. **Links that say "click here."**
8. **Using `noindex` or `robots.txt` as if it were security.**
9. **Duplicate pages with no canonical.**
10. **Content that only appears after heavy scripting,** with no fallback.
11. **Chasing tricks,** or paying for "guaranteed rankings."
12. **Never checking Search Console** or what is really indexed.

## Key Takeaways

- **SEO helps search engines find, understand, and show your pages.** Good HTML is where it starts.
- **Search engines crawl, index, rank, and show.** Different tags help at different stages.
- **Titles, descriptions, headings, and links** are your core tools.
- **Canonical, robots, and structured data** guide how search engines treat your pages.
- **Open Graph and Twitter Cards** shape how links look when shared.
- **Content quality and page experience** matter more than any tag.
- **No one can promise rankings.** Beware of tricks and scams.
- **Write for people first,** and tell the truth.
