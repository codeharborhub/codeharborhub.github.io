Some questions in a form only make sense **together**. "Standard," "Express," and "Pick up" are not three separate questions: they are three answers to one question, "How should we deliver it?" A date of birth is three boxes (day, month, and year) that answer one question. A billing address is a set of fields that belong to each other.

A sighted visitor sees these groups from the layout. But a screen reader user, who moves through the form one control at a time, has no layout to look at. The `<fieldset>` and `<legend>` elements fix that. They group related controls, and give the group a **name** that is read out with every control inside it. In this lesson, you will learn how they work, when to use them, and how to switch off or restyle a whole group.

By default, browsers draw a **thin box** around the fieldset, with the legend text sitting in the top border. You can restyle all of that with CSS, as you will see.

The rules:

| Element | What it is | Rules |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| `<fieldset>` | A group of related form controls | Goes inside (or next to) a form. Can contain any form controls, labels, text, and other elements |
| `<legend>` | The **caption** (the name) of its fieldset | Must be the **first child** of the `<fieldset>`. Use only one per fieldset |

## Why Groups Matter for Screen Readers

Compare how a screen reader presents the same radio buttons with and without a group.

**Without a fieldset**, a listener hears only:

> "Standard (5 days), radio button, 1 of 2, selected."
> "Express (2 days), radio button, 2 of 2."

Standard what? A speed? A price? A plan? The question has been lost.

**With a fieldset and legend**, they hear:

> "Delivery speed, group. Standard (5 days), radio button, 1 of 2, selected."

The legend gives the **question**, and each control gives an **answer**. This is the single most important reason to learn this lesson.

:::tip The rule of thumb
**Every group of radio buttons needs a `<fieldset>` and a `<legend>`.** A group of checkboxes that answer one question needs one too. Each individual control still needs its own `<label>`. The legend does not replace those labels.
:::

## Choosing Between a Label and a Legend

| For... | Use |
| :-- | :-- |
| **One** control (a text box, a select, a single checkbox) | A [`<label>`](./label-element) |
| A **group** of related controls that answer one question | A `<fieldset>` with a `<legend>` (and a `<label>` for each control inside) |

The legend is the question. The labels are the answers, or the names of the parts.

## Good Uses

### 1. Radio button groups

You saw this above. The legend is the question, and each radio has its own label.

### 2. Checkbox groups

```html
<fieldset>
  <legend>Which languages do you know?</legend>

  <label><input type="checkbox" name="lang" value="html" /> HTML</label>
  <label><input type="checkbox" name="lang" value="css" /> CSS</label>
  <label><input type="checkbox" name="lang" value="js" /> JavaScript</label>
</fieldset>
```

A **single** "I agree" checkbox needs only a label. A fieldset would be overkill.

### 3. Split fields that make one answer

A date of birth made of three boxes is one question:

```html
<fieldset>
  <legend>Date of birth</legend>

  <label for="dob-day">Day</label>
  <input type="text" id="dob-day" name="dob-day" inputmode="numeric" autocomplete="bday-day" size="3" />

  <label for="dob-month">Month</label>
  <input type="text" id="dob-month" name="dob-month" inputmode="numeric" autocomplete="bday-month" size="3" />

  <label for="dob-year">Year</label>
  <input type="text" id="dob-year" name="dob-year" inputmode="numeric" autocomplete="bday-year" size="5" />
</fieldset>
```

A screen reader says "Date of birth, group. Day, edit text." The same idea works for a card expiry date (month and year), or a phone number split into parts.

### 4. A block of related fields

An address, a payment section, or a "Your details" step:

```html
<fieldset>
  <legend>Shipping address</legend>

  <label for="street">Street</label>
  <input type="text" id="street" name="street" autocomplete="shipping street-address" />

  <label for="city">City</label>
  <input type="text" id="city" name="city" autocomplete="shipping address-level2" />

  <label for="postcode">Postal code</label>
  <input type="text" id="postcode" name="postcode" autocomplete="shipping postal-code" />
</fieldset>
```

When a form has **two** similar blocks (shipping and billing), the legend tells them apart. Without it, the screen reader would say "Street, edit text" twice with no clue which is which.

### 5. When not to use it

Do **not** wrap every pair of fields, or a whole short form, in a fieldset. A form with three text boxes (name, email, message) needs no groups. Too many groups make a form tiring to listen to, because the legend is announced each time the visitor enters a group. Use fieldsets where they **add meaning**.

## Try It: Disabling a Whole Group

A `<fieldset>` can have the **`disabled`** attribute, which switches off **every control inside it** in one go. It is a handy way to turn off a whole section, such as a billing address that is the same as the shipping address.

Try it:

- With the box **ticked**, the billing group is greyed out. Press Submit, and only `ship_street` is sent. The disabled `required` field does **not** block the form.
- **Untick** the box, and the billing group comes alive. Now the required field must be filled in, and `bill_street` is sent.

What a `disabled` fieldset does:

| Effect | Detail |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Controls are greyed out** | Inputs, selects, textareas, and buttons inside cannot be used or focused |
| **Values are not sent** | Disabled controls are left out of the form data |
| **Validation is skipped** | A disabled `required` field cannot block the form |
| **Applies to all descendants** | Even deeply nested controls |
| **Exception** | Controls inside the fieldset's **first `<legend>`** stay enabled |

:::note Disabled is not hidden
A disabled group is still **visible** (and still read out by screen readers, as "dimmed" or "unavailable"). If a section should disappear entirely, hide it too (with the `hidden` attribute on a wrapper), and keep the fieldset `disabled` so its values are not sent and not validated.
:::

If you do not want to **grey out** the controls, but just want to hide the group and skip it, use `hidden` **and** `disabled` together:

```js
section.hidden = true;
fieldset.disabled = true;
```

## Other Attributes

| Attribute | What it does | Example |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| `disabled` | Disables all controls inside | `disabled` |
| `name` | A name for scripts (it is **not** sent to the server) | `name="billing"` |
| `form` | Connects the fieldset to a form it is not inside | `form="checkout"` |

The `name` lets you reach the group in JavaScript: `form.elements.billing`, and read its `elements` list. The fieldset itself has no value and sends nothing.

## Styling Fieldset and Legend

The default look (a box with a title in the border) is old fashioned, and has some odd rules. Many designs reset it, and then restyle.

### A Clean Reset

```css title="style.css"
fieldset {
  min-width: 0;           /* fixes a bug where fieldsets refuse to shrink on small screens */
  margin: 0 0 1.5rem;
  padding: 0;
  border: 0;
}

legend {
  padding: 0;
  margin-bottom: 0.5rem;
  font-weight: 700;
  font-size: 1.1rem;
}
```

`min-width: 0` matters, because by default a fieldset will not become narrower than its content, which can cause a horizontal scroll on phones.

### A Card Style

```css
fieldset {
  min-width: 0;
  margin: 0 0 1.5rem;
  padding: 1rem 1.25rem 1.25rem;
  border: 1px solid #cbd5e1;
  border-radius: 8px;
}

legend {
  padding: 0 0.5rem;      /* space around the title in the border */
  font-weight: 700;
}
```

### Things to Know

- **A legend is a special element.** It sits in the fieldset's top border by default, and it is hard to move with normal CSS (it ignores `float` and many `position` tricks, and `display: flex` on a fieldset has been buggy in some browsers).
- If you want a **full control of layout**, put a wrapper `<div>` inside the fieldset, and apply `display: grid` or `flex` to that.
- To make the legend behave like a normal heading, a common trick is `legend { float: left; width: 100%; }` followed by a `legend + * { clear: both; }`. It works in current browsers, but test it. Often, the plain reset above is enough.
- You may put a **heading inside the legend**, for a group that is also a section of the page:

```html
<fieldset>
  <legend><h2>Payment details</h2></legend>
  ...
</fieldset>
```

This is allowed, and screen readers announce both the group name and the heading. Use it sparingly, and only when the legend is really a section heading.

- **Do not remove the legend visually** with `display: none`, because screen readers then lose the group name. If you want it hidden on screen, use a **visually hidden** style (see below). But a visible question is nearly always better, since sighted people benefit from it too.

```css
.visually-hidden {
  position: absolute;
  width: 1px;
  height: 1px;
  margin: -1px;
  padding: 0;
  overflow: hidden;
  clip: rect(0 0 0 0);
  white-space: nowrap;
  border: 0;
}
```

```html
<fieldset>
  <legend class="visually-hidden">Delivery speed</legend>
  ...
</fieldset>
```

## Nesting Fieldsets

You can put a fieldset inside another, for example, "Payment" containing "Card expiry." Be careful:

- Screen readers announce **both** legends, so the listener hears "Payment, group. Card expiry, group. Month..." Two levels are the most you should use.
- Deep nesting is tiring to listen to, and hard to style.
- If you find you need three levels, the form is probably too complex. Break it into **steps** or pages.

```html
<fieldset>
  <legend>Payment</legend>

  <label for="card">Card number</label>
  <input type="text" id="card" name="card" inputmode="numeric" autocomplete="cc-number" />

  <fieldset>
    <legend>Expiry date</legend>
    <label for="exp-m">Month</label> <input type="text" id="exp-m" name="exp-m" size="3" autocomplete="cc-exp-month" />
    <label for="exp-y">Year</label> <input type="text" id="exp-y" name="exp-y" size="5" autocomplete="cc-exp-year" />
  </fieldset>
</fieldset>
```

## Alternatives: `role="group"` and `aria-labelledby`

Sometimes you cannot use a `<fieldset>`, for example, in a layout that fights its styling, or in a component library. A `<div>` with `role="group"` and a label does the same job for assistive technology:

```html
<div role="group" aria-labelledby="speed-title">
  <p id="speed-title">Delivery speed</p>
  <label><input type="radio" name="speed" value="standard" /> Standard</label>
  <label><input type="radio" name="speed" value="express" /> Express</label>
</div>
```

For a set of radio buttons, `role="radiogroup"` is the more exact role.

But a real `<fieldset>` is better when you can use one, since it is built into the browser, works everywhere, and needs no extra attributes. And, only the `<fieldset>` can **disable** its contents. ARIA never changes the behaviour of controls, it only describes them.

## Accessibility at a Glance

- **Group related controls** (especially radios and checkboxes) with `<fieldset>` and `<legend>`.
- **Make the legend a clear question or title:** "Delivery speed," "Date of birth," "Which languages do you know?" It is read before **every** control in the group, so keep it **short**.
- **Keep a label on every control inside.** The legend does not replace labels.
- **Use the legend as the first child.** If it is anywhere else, browsers may not treat it as the group's caption.
- **Do not hide legends** with `display: none` or `visibility: hidden`. Use a visually hidden style if you must.
- **Do not overuse groups,** or nest them deeply.
- **Do not use fieldset for layout.** Use it for **meaning**, and use CSS for appearance.
- **For `disabled` groups,** explain why a section is unavailable ("Same as shipping address") so that visitors understand.
- **Make required groups clear.** For a required radio group, say so in the legend text or help text ("Delivery speed (required)"), and put `required` on the radios.
- **Show group-level errors** near the legend, and link them with `aria-describedby` on the fieldset: "Choose a delivery speed to continue."

```html
<fieldset aria-describedby="speed-error">
  <legend>Delivery speed</legend>
  ...
  <p id="speed-error">Choose a delivery speed to continue.</p>
</fieldset>
```

## Security Notes

- **A disabled fieldset is not a security tool.** Anyone can send a value for a "disabled" field by hand, so the server must decide which fields to **accept**, and validate them.
- **Do not trust the `name` of a fieldset,** since it is never sent.
- **Treat every field in a group as untrusted,** as always.

See [Secure Form Design](./secure-form-design).

## Global Audience Notes

- **Keep legends short and plain,** so that they translate well, and are quick to hear.
- **Translated legends can be longer.** Do not set a fixed width on the legend.
- **Right-to-left pages** mirror the legend and the border automatically with `dir="rtl"`.
- **Do not rely on a visual box** for grouping. In some cultures and reading systems, the layout is read differently, and the **text** of the legend is what is always understood.
- **Date and address groups** differ between countries (the order of day and month, the parts of an address). Let the legend say what is asked, and choose the fields with your audience in mind.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Radio buttons with no fieldset and legend.**
2. **Putting the `<legend>` anywhere except first,** or using more than one.
3. **Using a legend instead of labels,** so individual controls have no names.
4. **Wrapping every field in its own fieldset.**
5. **Deeply nested fieldsets,** which are tiring to hear.
6. **Hiding the legend** with `display: none`.
7. **Using `<fieldset>` for page layout,** instead of grouping by meaning.
8. **Writing long legends,** which are read for every control.
9. **Forgetting `min-width: 0`,** so the fieldset causes horizontal scrolling on phones.
10. **Disabling a group with no explanation.**
11. **Expecting a disabled fieldset's values to be sent.**
12. **Trusting a disabled fieldset** to stop a value being submitted by hand.

## Key Takeaways

- `<fieldset>` groups related controls, and `<legend>` (its first child) names the group. Screen readers read the legend with every control inside.
- Every radio group, and every checkbox group that answers one question, needs a fieldset and a legend. Each control still needs its own label.
- Use groups for meaning: option sets, split date fields, and blocks like shipping and billing addresses. Do not wrap everything, and avoid deep nesting.
- `disabled` on a fieldset switches off all controls inside: they are greyed out, not sent, and skip validation.
- Reset the default style with `border: 0`, `padding: 0`, and `min-width: 0`, or build a card style. Never hide the legend with `display: none`.
- Keep legends short and clear, and always validate on the server.
